# Consumer protection changes — September 27, 2026

Scope: the eight primary findings in `legal-exposure-review-2026-09-27.md`, for a California business serving U.S. customers. These are local application changes, not a deployment or a finding that the business meets every legal requirement.

## Evidence and retention limits

- No historical detailed listing snapshot is backfilled from today's listing and represented as purchase-time evidence. Prior orders show when no snapshot exists.
- Original accepted preorder terms remain authoritative if a later balance checkout also captures a newer listing. Compare the two when reviewing a material change.
- Managed R2 listing photos are retained. External URLs and bundled static image paths are recorded references; their underlying bytes are not automatically copied into a private archive. Preserve licensed originals and deployment artifacts separately.
- Detached retained media is served only through the authorized evidence API; previously public copies may remain in browser/CDN caches. Retention cannot retract already downloaded content.
- Evidence, recovery identities, notices and audit records require restricted access and a documented retention/legal-hold process. Immutable triggers prevent routine deletion; eventual disposal needs a controlled migration after retention review. Monitor the existing storage budget.
- Legacy abandoned preorder deposits are reviewed individually. Automatic transfer remains disabled; there is no new “approve forfeiture” shortcut. Already transferred amounts are reversed when the existing refund machinery requires it.

## Shipping-label update — November 5, 2026

- Collector sellers can create and reprint labels in My Garage → My Sales using the same tools as professional sellers. Checkout shipping quotes determine the buyer's shipping charge; they do not purchase a label. Sellers confirm the packed parcel before buying.
- New confirmed label purchases record the displayed carrier rate in `orders.shipping_label_cost_cents`. The deduction and shipment are saved together. Combined orders share one label cost, allocated in cents by their available proceeds; reprinting creates no new charge or deduction. Earlier labels retain their existing accounting.
- Payouts deduct recorded postage after proportional customer-refund allocation. Customer refunds are not reduced by label costs. Seller proceeds cannot become negative, so costs exceeding proceeds after a later refund remain an MCC reconciliation item.
- Paid orders can fund labels before Stripe publishes its processing fee. Rate and purchase requests refresh missing fees for the selected, owned orders without releasing payouts. Until fees are available, the spending limit excludes tax, marketplace fees, known processing fees, refunded money and existing postage; this provisional limit is not recorded as final proceeds. Final payouts still require actual fees and deduct the saved label cost once. A later fee or refund exceeding remaining proceeds requires MCC reconciliation.
- Legacy direct payouts and already released proceeds cannot fund new labels. Overlapping quotes are serialized. An ambiguous carrier response leaves the purchase blocked for support reconciliation; do not reset it or buy again until Shippo confirms the transaction outcome.
- The automatic deduction covers the displayed label rate. Separately billed insurance, subscription fees, carrier adjustments, and carrier label refunds still require reconciliation; this change does not import Shippo invoices. See [Shippo billing](https://support.goshippo.com/hc/en-us/articles/360003859092-How-Billing-Works-in-Shippo).
- Migration `0040_shipping_label_costs.sql` adds a constant-zero default without backfilling historical charges. Tests use mocked carrier, email, and payout providers; they do not purchase real labels or transfer funds.
